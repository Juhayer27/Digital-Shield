What helps most?

A practical password should be hard to guess and unique to each account.

Length matters

Longer passwords and passphrases are generally harder to guess than short, complicated-looking ones.

Avoid predictable patterns

Names, birthdays, keyboard sequences, common words, and simple substitutions are easy to guess.

Use a unique password

Never reuse a password across important accounts. A reputable password manager can help.

This checker uses local heuristics only. It does not query breached-password databases or calculate a verified crack time.

Generation safety

A few important notes about this tool.

Secure randomness

Uses the browser's built-in crypto.getRandomValues() instead of Math.random().

Balanced selection

Uses rejection sampling when mapping random bytes to characters to avoid modulo bias, and includes each selected character group.

Handle with care

Copy only to a trusted password manager. Clipboard access depends on browser permissions and secure context.

This tool does not store generated passwords. Clearing or navigating away removes the current output from the page.

Learn & protect / 03

Browser security guide

Practical, browser-independent advice to help you browse with more confidence.

Beginner friendly

01. Keep your browser and operating system updated

Updates often fix security flaws. Enable automatic updates where practical and install updates from official device settings or vendor websites.

Action: Check for updates on your device today.

02. Understand HTTPS — but don't over-trust it

HTTPS encrypts data in transit between your browser and a website. It does not prove that a website is honest; phishing sites can also use HTTPS.

Action: Verify the domain and context before entering sensitive information.

03. Use multifactor authentication

Where possible, enable passkeys or an authenticator app. Save recovery codes in a secure place. SMS codes can be better than no second factor, but may be more vulnerable than phishing-resistant options.

Action: Secure your primary email and financial accounts first.

04. Be selective with extensions

Extensions can read or modify data on websites depending on their permissions. Install only what you need, check the publisher, and remove unused extensions.

Action: Review your extension list and permissions.

05. Manage cookies and site permissions

Review location, camera, microphone, notification, and pop-up permissions. Blocking all cookies can break sites, so choose settings that fit your needs.

Action: Remove permissions for sites you no longer use.

06. Treat public Wi-Fi carefully

Prefer HTTPS websites, keep device sharing turned off on unfamiliar networks, and avoid ignoring certificate warnings. A VPN does not make phishing or malware harmless.

Action: Turn off automatic connection to unknown Wi-Fi networks.

07. Check links without opening them

On many devices, you can long-press a link to preview its destination. Watch for misspellings, unexpected subdomains, URL shorteners, and domains that imitate familiar brands.

Action: For important accounts, open the official app or type the known address yourself.

08. Protect your device and recovery methods

Use a screen lock, keep backups, and know how to revoke lost-device sessions. Never share one-time codes or recovery codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Action: Confirm your device lock and account recovery options.
